How this model works

The heart is a 4-chamber section on a 1 mm grid (~9,000 cells). Each cell is excitable: when a neighbour fires it activates after distance / conduction velocity, unless it is still refractory. Tissues differ in velocity (AV node lumped at ~0.05 m/s, myocardium 0.5–0.9, Purkinje 2.6 m/s), action-potential duration, and connectivity — the fibrous annulus insulates atria from ventricles, bundle branches are insulated from septal muscle until their Purkinje terminals.

APD and conduction velocity follow restitution curves (shorter/slower when re-excited early), so QT shortens with rate and premature beats conduct slowly. Ventricular APD is longest in early-activated cells, which is why repolarization runs backwards and the T wave is upright.

The ECG is not drawn from a template: at every 2 ms the model sums the dipole sources (−∇Vm) of all cells into the lead field of each electrode (RA, LA, LL, V1–V6), forms Einthoven/Goldberger/Wilson leads and passes them through a 0.05–40 Hz filter. Bundle-branch blocks, delta waves, ST elevation, Q waves from scar and AV dissociation all emerge from the tissue. Chest leads are approximate because the model is a single plane.
